From Rating to Release

Feedback that turns into action, every week|Best Customer Feedback Strategy, Category 85

Bottom line

Every customer score gets heard.

From a single rating to a shipped fix.

At BrightMove, no piece of feedback is a dead end. A person reviews every negative score each week, traces it to a real fix and tells every user when it ships. Customers feel the difference, rating 94% of their conversations positive.

Section 1: How customers give feedback

In the submission: Achievements, How it works; Supporting materials, item 2

Shows: giving feedback asks little or nothing of the customer.

  • Customer experience score: Intercom's AI scores the conversation by reading it, so it asks nothing of the customer.
  • Customer satisfaction rating: an optional 1–5 star rating offered at the end of the same chat.
  • Feature requests can be made in the conversation the customer is already in; Wiz, the AI support agent, explains how, and a person logs the request.

Section 2: How the loop closes each week

In the submission: Achievements, How it works and What it produced; Supporting materials, items 3 and 4

Shows: every negative score is reviewed weekly, traced to a root cause, and acted on.

Each week BrightMove's support team reviews every negative experience score and satisfaction rating, plus the tagged account maintenance conversations. Each is traced to a root cause and acted on through an update to LightHub, BrightMove's online help center, a change to the written instructions Wiz follows (its guidance), or a product request. Customers whose request leads to a fix are told what changed; the questionnaire reply in Section 3 is an example. When large customers repeatedly ask for the same admin task, it becomes a candidate on the product to-do list for a feature customers can use themselves.

Section 3: When code can’t change today

In the submission: Achievements, What it produced; Supporting materials, item 5

Example: an undocumented questionnaire name limit (June 2026)

  1. Jun 26, 2026: the feedback. A customer reported that the Create Questionnaire button was greyed out with no explanation. The questionnaire name had passed a character limit that was not documented anywhere. The customer suggested blocking typing at the limit or making the limit longer.
  2. Review. The team noted that users could not tell when they had crossed the limit, and that the limit was not documented.
  3. Same morning: the fix. A character-limit note was added to the LightHub article Creating Interview Questions & Questionnaires. The product change was logged as not planned for now, to be revisited if the issue keeps coming up.
  4. About 90 minutes after the email arrived: the follow-up. A support team member told the customer: In an effort to clear up any confusion with the current functionality, I have added a character limit note to the article: Creating Interview Questions & Questionnaires. With our resources and initiatives in flight, this is not something we plan to move forward on just yet. If we continue to see an uptick in frustration around this specific area of the ATS, we will revisit your request. We appreciate you bringing it to us, as always, as your suggestions have helped us shape the ATS for the better, time and time again.

Not every request can move ahead of planned development work. When a code change isn’t possible today, the team still closes the gap where it can: in the LightHub articles customers and Wiz rely on.

Section 4: How the loop moves the product

In the submission: Achievements, What it produced

Case: drag-and-drop status moves in BrightFlow

In the submission: Supporting materials, item 6

Shows: a long-standing customer request moving from the product to-do list to release, with customers told inside the software when it shipped.

A partner first asked in January 2025 for a way to move candidates between columns in BrightFlow, BrightMove's board view that shows candidates in columns by hiring stage, without a separate status-update step. At the time the request was closed because it was judged to need a full redesign of the view. In March 2026 a support team member checked with the partner on whether it was still a priority. In August 2026 a customer raised the same friction as a blocker to wider adoption by her team, and the request was reopened as a top-priority item on the product to-do list. The team built spots inside each column where a candidate can be dropped onto a status, a pop-up window that fills in activity details by default, and a company setting that skips the pop-up. After repeated rounds of quality testing, including in a pre-release copy of the system, it shipped in late October 2026 (release 2026.13.0).

Customers were told through the “What’s New” post sent with the release. The team began publishing a What’s New post with every release so that customers learn about releases inside the software, after earlier releases had left the customers who asked for them without notice. The post for this release, “What’s New Deployment 2026.13.0,” is shown inside the software, on the home screen, to every customer at sign-in. Its first item reads: Move candidates with a drag and a drop. In BrightFlow, drop a candidate’s card right into the status you want and the status updates instantly. Companies can also turn on the BrightFlow Bypass Activity Modal preference so moves save on the spot with default activity details. No pop-up needed!

  1. Jan 2025: partner request logged; closed as requiring a redesign.
  2. Mar 27, 2026: a support team member asks the partner whether it is still a priority.
  3. Aug 25, 2026: reopened as a priority item on the product to-do list after a customer request.
  4. Oct 13–23, 2026: built, then put through rounds of quality testing; final pre-release test Oct 23.
  5. Late October 2026: shipped in release 2026.13.0; What’s New post set live around Oct 23.

Two more shipped changes

In the submission: Supporting materials, item 7

Shows: the loop reaches the product to-do list repeatedly.

Show Password

An executive at a staffing customer was locked out on Feb 10, 2026 and, while working through it in chat, wrote: I wish there was a eye to look at the password. A support team member flagged it internally as a possible enhancement, and a ticket was opened that afternoon.

Dates: chat Feb 10, 2026; ticket Feb 10, 2026; resolved and tested Apr 7, 2026. Source: Intercom and the ticket record.

"Required Correct" indicator

A customer asked in chat on Mar 26, 2026 why a referred candidate had been auto-declined: It worries me that the system is rejecting qualified candidates. A support team member found two versions of the same screening question, one marked Required Correct (a setting that requires a correct answer from the candidate) and one not, and that the Required Correct version was likely the one assigned. The fix adds a Required Correct badge next to those questions when assigning them to a job.

Dates: chat Mar 26, 2026; ticket Apr 1, 2026; resolved Apr 20, 2026; tested Apr 22, 2026. Source: Intercom and the ticket record.

Coverage beyond surveys

The experience score covers conversations whether or not the customer responds to a survey, so BrightMove hears from customers who would never fill one out: 3,272 scored conversations versus 438 satisfaction ratings, about 7.5 times as many.
